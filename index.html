From your rocket
to a satellite that is looked after.

Bring a rocket and a satellite. CRIC tells you whether it reaches orbit and what to change if it does not, when and where to launch around weather and other satellites, and then operates the satellite among 18,806 tracked objects, with an AI that does the work and knows when to ask a person.

STEP 1 · YOUR ROCKET

Does it reach orbit?

Pick a published launcher, drop a spec sheet photo, or type your own numbers. A 3-DOF ascent flies it; if it falls short, every suggested fix is flown again before you see it.

  • Nemotron 3.5 Super VL reads the spec sheet; each number tagged read / derived / assumed
  • Stage impact areas from 500 dispersed flights
STEP 2 · WHEN AND WHERE

Pick the launch minute

Seven days of weather and space weather for every site that can reach the orbit, then each liftoff minute screened against the catalog. One minute can pass a satellite at under a kilometre; a few minutes later is clear.

  • Weather, range and orbital-safety votes by Nemotron 3 Super
  • Mission director: Nemotron 3 Ultra, unable to pick a vetoed window
STEP 3 · OPERATE

Look after it

The satellite joins the catalog. CRIC tells you when it passes over home, screens it for close approaches, and runs the same decision pipeline as the console, ending with your approval.

  • Collision probability verified against NASA CARA test cases
  • Two routes must agree before any burn; otherwise a person decides

WHAT IS REAL, WHAT IS NOT

Real data, real models

  • CelesTrak orbits (SGP4), NOAA SWPC space weather, Open-Meteo forecasts
  • Published launcher figures, each number labelled with its source
  • NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Nano, Super, Ultra and 3.5 Super VL on Nebius Token Factory; Tavily for web evidence
  • Ascent model checked against published capacities: −2 % to −20 % (conservative)

Stated plainly

  • Garage One and JIMIN-1 are fictional; the launched satellite exists only in the simulation
  • Launch rules and dispersion scatter are our assumptions, not a range's
  • Rocket shapes are derived from the numbers; plume, smoke and sky are illustration
  • CRIC never commands a spacecraft. All limits
